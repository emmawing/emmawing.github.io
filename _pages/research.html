Cognitive hierarchies: Mental categories and their structure

Why do the same categories, in the same hierarchical sequence, emerge repeatedly across cognition? I investigate the hypothesis that they appear in language, concepts, and the brain, and why that might be.


SYNTAX: a hierarchy of grammatical categories (the syntactic functional hierarchy) in which the verb and its arguments are nested in tense and aspect phrases, which are themselves nested in clause- and discourse-marking phrases (Chomsky, 1995)

SEMANTICS a hierarchy of categories of linguistic meaning from event (kind)s, to event situated in a time and world (token), to situations related to speakers and anchored to a discourse (Ramchand & Svenonius, 2014)

BRAIN: a hierarchy of embedded, interconnected networks of functional connectivity extending from segregated regions dependent on external stimuli to integrated regions independent of external stimuli (the Sensorimotor to Association axis), as well as in these regions' associated functions (the functional hierarchy): sensation and perception, working memory and attention, and social cognition, narrative, and internal mentation (Margulies et al., 2023)

CONCEPTS: a hierarchy of conceptual categories increasinly abstracted away from sensorimotor representations, to those connecting external to internal to thoughts, to entirely internal thoughts independent of sensorimotor experience (Wing & Tabor, in prep)
