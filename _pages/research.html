--- layout: archive title: permalink: /research/ author_profile: true --- Through intentional acts of bridging across areas in cognitive science, I have observed that the same broad categories, in the same hierarchical sequence, emerge repeatedly across cognition. • in syntax, the sequence surfaces as a hierarchy of grammatical categories (the syntactic functional hierarchy) in which the verb and its arguments are nested in tense and aspect phrases, which are themselves nested in clause- and discourse-marking phrases (Chomsky, 1995). • in semantics as event (kind)s, event situated in time (token) to situation linked to speakers and discourse (Ramchand & Svenonius, 2014). • in the brain I argue that the sequence arises in hierarchically embedded networks of functional connectivity extending from segregated regions dependent on external stimuli to integrated regions independent of external stimuli (the sensorimotor to association axis), as well as in these regions’ associated functions (the functional hierarchy): sensation and perception, working memory and attention, and social cognition, narrative, and internal mentation (Margulies et al., 2023). • in concepts as abstraction away from sensorimotor representations, to those connecting external to internal to thoughts, to entirely internal thoughts independent of sensorimotor experience.
