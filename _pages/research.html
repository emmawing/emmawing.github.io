Mental categories and their structure

Why do the same categories, in the same hierarchical sequence, emerge repeatedly across cognition? I investigate the hypothesis that the same categories appear across language, concepts, and the brain, and why that might be.


in syntax, the sequence surfaces as a hierarchy of grammatical categories (the syntactic functional hierarchy) in which the verb and its arguments are nested in tense and aspect phrases, which are themselves nested in clause- and discourse-marking phrases (Chomsky, 1995)

in semantics event (kind)s, event situated in a time and world (token), situation anchored to speakers and discourse (Ramchand & Svenonius, 2014)

BRAIN: a hierarchically embedded networks of functional connectivity extending from segregated regions dependent on external stimuli to integrated regions independent of external stimuli (the Sensorimotor to Association axis), as well as in these regions' associated functions (the functional hierarchy): sensation and perception, working memory and attention, and social cognition, narrative, and internal mentation (Margulies et al., 2023)

CONCEPTS: abstraction away from sensorimotor representations, to those connecting external to internal to thoughts, to entirely internal thoughts independent of sensorimotor experience (Wing & Tabor, in prep)
